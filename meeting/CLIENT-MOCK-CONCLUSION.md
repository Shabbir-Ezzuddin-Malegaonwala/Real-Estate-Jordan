# Client mock — conclusion (skeptical-CEO stress test)

We ran the pitch against a hostile-but-fair CEO persona (Ahmad Al-Khatib, ~70 units
in Dabouq/Khalda) before the real meeting. This is the outcome and the playbook it
produced. **Purpose: know exactly which pitch survives scrutiny, and what closes.**

## The verdict he reached
**A firm YES to a paid pilot — NOT yet a purchase.** "You've earned the pilot. You
have not yet earned the purchase." He committed to hand over **four** of his own past
sold-out buildings (two that cleared fast, two that dragged) for a **blind back-test**,
under NDA, next week.

## What EARNED the yes (do more of this)
1. **Honesty about limits beat every feature.** The single most persuasive move was
   refusing to invent numbers — admitting the calibration is market-wide (not yet
   segmented), and admitting **no real sold-price accuracy figure can exist in Jordan**
   because no one publishes unit transaction prices. His words: *"the vendors who
   admitted their limits were the ones who didn't blow up my budget."*
2. **The method, told transparently:** comparable-sales (how a good appraiser works,
   at scale + consistently), **bounded by a cost floor (DoS build cost) and an income
   ceiling (area yield)**, confidence stated openly, every number provenance-stamped.
   "I can see inside it" mattered — not a black box.
3. **Volunteering to be graded on his own data.** Offering a blind back-test on his
   buildings — and setting the goalpost *against his own free option* (**beat his
   brokers' original estimates or he pays nothing beyond the pilot fee**) — is what
   flipped him from skeptic to buyer. "A vendor who volunteers to be measured against
   the free option I already have… has actually thought about whether his product works."

## What nearly LOST it (the trap to avoid in the real meeting)
- **Answering a commercial question with a feature list.** When he asked cost /
  timeline / pass-band, we drifted into product features. He caught it instantly:
  *"That's the salesman slipping back in at the finish line, and I notice it every
  time."* → In the real meeting, when asked a number, give the number (or say who
  owns it and when it'll be on paper). Do not deflect to features.

## The concrete close (the offer that works)
A **small, fixed-fee, blind back-test pilot** on the client's own past sold-out
buildings, with everything in writing up front:
- **Blind:** we price every unit before seeing their closing prices; prices sealed
  until our numbers are locked, then revealed side by side.
- **Goalposts, agreed before the ball is kicked (non-renegotiable after):**
  median unit within **~10%**, building-average within **~7–8%**, **and beat the
  client's brokers' original estimates** on the same buildings.
- **Data he gives:** unit attributes he already holds (location, size, beds, floor,
  finish, view, parking) + the sealed actual closing prices.
- **Timeline:** ~2–3 weeks from handover to the per-unit / per-building error report.
- **Cost:** an **exact** fixed pilot fee (Mohammad sets the number — not a range;
  he will not sign against "low four figures").
- **Data ownership:** NDA + segregated storage, his account only, never pooled —
  and the comparable-sales method means his data isn't absorbed into a model that
  then prices competitors.

## Objections we must be ready to answer live
1. **"What's your accuracy?"** → Honest: no sold-price back-test exists in Jordan
   (no published transaction prices). We validate three ways — triangulated by the
   cost floor + income ceiling, cross-checked vs government indices, and *provable*
   only on the client's own data via the pilot. Never quote a fabricated %.
2. **"Why should I trust the calibration?"** → It's deliberately conservative,
   transparent line-by-line, anchored by floor/ceiling, and sharpens with the
   client's own closing data.
3. **"Is my data safe?"** → NDA, segregated, only their account, method doesn't
   absorb their data into a competitor-facing model.
4. **"Is it current?"** → Today it's a real, dated snapshot; automatic-refresh is
   **the next production step** (present as not-yet-built, per manager's rule).

## One-line takeaway for the meeting
> Sell the **honesty and the transparent method**, not a magic number — and close on
> a **paid blind pilot graded against the client's own buildings and their own
> brokers.** That is the pitch that survived the hardest CEO in the room.

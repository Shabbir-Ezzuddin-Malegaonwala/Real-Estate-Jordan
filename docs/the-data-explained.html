First, the problem

A builder needs to know one thing: what price should I sell my homes at?

In Jordan, nobody publishes what homes actually sold for. So we can't just look up the answer. We have to work it out from lots of clues — that's what all this data is for.
The big picture

We collect 6 kinds of information

Think of it like clues a detective gathers. No single clue gives the answer — together they do.

Clue #1 — the main one

Homes for sale

52,000homes we collected
6websites (Bayut, OpenSooq…)

We gather real homes listed for sale on popular websites. This is the biggest clue — the prices of homes near yours tell us what yours is worth.

Important: these are asking prices (what sellers want), not final sale prices. Sellers usually get a bit less — so we lower the number a little to be realistic.
Clue #1 — what we know about each home

Being honest: some details (like the floor number or how old the building is) are often missing. So we lean on the things we do know — price, size, and location — and we say when we're less sure.
🧱
Clue #2

How much it costs to build

This comes from the government (the Department of Statistics). It tells us the cost of building one square metre.

Why it matters: you should never sell for less than it costs to build — you'd lose money. So this sets the lowest safe price.
🟩
Clue #3

Land prices

What land actually costs in different areas.

Why it matters: it helps answer "how much can I pay for the land and still make a profit?"
🏦
Clue #4

How much rent brings in

For each area, how much money a home earns from renting it out.

Why it matters: some buyers are investors — they'll only pay a price that gives them good rent. This helps set the top price.
🏗️
Clue #5

Other projects nearby

Other new buildings being sold in the area, and their prices.

Why it matters: these are your competition. If ten towers are selling next door, that changes your price.
📰
Clue #6

Market news

Recent, dated news — interest rates, new roads and metro lines, government rules, buying activity.

Why it matters: the world changes. A new metro line or a rule change can push prices up. This is the "what changed?" signal.
Plus, for the map

Places & roads nearby 🗺️

86,000nearby places (schools, malls, clinics…)

We also collected tens of thousands of nearby places and roads. Homes near good schools, malls and transport are usually worth more.

Being honest

What this data can't do

🏷️

Asking, not sold

We know what sellers ask, not the final handshake price. We adjust for this.

📉

Thin in small areas

Big areas have lots of homes; small ones have very few. We only price where we have enough.

📅

Gaps in time

Home dates cluster in a few periods, so "price over time" is rough, not perfect.

🙅

Never a guess

When we don't have enough, we say "no data yet" — we never make up a number.

The big idea

We price your home the way a good appraiser does — by looking at similar homes nearby and what they're going for.

No magic, no AI guessing. Every number comes from real listings you can see. If we can't back it up with real homes, we won't make it up.

1

Find comparable homes

We look for homes that are like yours: same area, similar size, similar number of bedrooms, and listed recently.

Builders call these "comparables" — or just "comps". A 3-bed apartment in Abdoun is only fairly compared to other 3-beds in Abdoun, not to a villa across the city.
2

Price per square metre

A big home costs more than a small one — that tells us nothing. So we divide the price by the size to compare apples to apples.

200,000 JD ÷ 150 m² → 1,333/m²
280,000 JD ÷ 200 m² → 1,400/m²

Now we can line homes up side by side. Everything from here on is measured per square metre.

Before we do anything, we trim the outliers — the crazy-high and crazy-low ones. We keep the sensible middle band, so one broken listing can never ruin your price.
5

Nudge each comp to match yours

No two homes are identical. A comp on a higher floor, or with a nicer finish, is worth a little more — so we adjust it up or down to be "like yours".

Every nudge is small and comes from the market itself — there's no hard-coded "a view is worth +8%". And we only adjust where we actually have the data.
The golden rule

When in doubt, we don't guess

If we don't know a home's floor or finish, we simply don't adjust for it — and if the whole area is too thin, we say "not enough data".

A missing detail is never treated as a made-up number. Honest gaps beat confident guesses.
The safety net below

The cost floor

Using government build-cost data, we know roughly what it costs to build one square metre. You should never sell below that — you'd lose money.

This sets the lowest safe price. If nearby homes ever dip under the cost to build, the floor quietly lifts your number back up to safe ground.
The ceiling above

The income ceiling

Some buyers are investors. They'll only pay a price the rent can justify. Using area rents, we work out the most a rent-focused buyer would sensibly pay.

This helps set the top price. Rent divided by the yield investors expect gives a fair ceiling — push above it and the investors walk away.
Floor below, ceiling above

Two guard rails

Cost floor

From government build costs. The lowest price that still makes you money. Holds the bottom up.

Income ceiling

From area rents. The most an investor will pay. Caps the top.

The nearby homes give the real price. The floor and ceiling keep it honest at both ends.

How much to trust it

Very sure, fairly sure, or not sure

Every price comes with a confidence badge. It's based on two simple things:

How many comps we found

More similar homes → more sure.

How alike they are

Prices that agree → more sure.

Less sure = wider band. When comps are few or scattered, we widen the range on purpose — never a fake tight number.
Bonus: "where to build"

Ranking areas fairly

To score which area is a good bet, we don't compare raw prices — one broken 120,000/m² row would break the scale.

Instead we ask "where does this area rank against all the others?" (its percentile). The best area is 100, the worst is 0, and one crazy outlier can't distort anyone.
